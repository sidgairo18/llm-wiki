## Summary

DiffusionEngine (DE) repurposes a frozen pre-trained Latent Diffusion Model as a scalable data engine for object detection. Rather than training a separate generator, the authors attach a lightweight **Detection Adapter** (DA) to a frozen Stable Diffusion backbone that aligns implicit semantic/positional features inside the diffusion U-Net to bounding-box predictions. This produces high-quality image–box pairs in a single forward pass, enabling generation of large-scale annotated detection datasets (COCO-DE, VOC-DE) used to substantially boost downstream detectors.

## Key Contributions

- A unified, single-stage data engine for detection: an off-the-shelf LDM plus a small detection adapter yields scalable, diverse, and well-annotated training pairs.
- Detection Adapter that learns to map multi-scale U-Net features (from selected denoising timesteps) to object boxes/categories without finetuning the diffusion backbone.
- Two new scaled detection datasets, COCO-DE and VOC-DE, generated by DE.
- Consistent improvements across architectures (Faster R-CNN, RetinaNet, FCOS, DINO, YOLOX) and settings (low-data, full-data, cross-domain, label-efficient).

## Technical Details

**Backbone.** A frozen Stable Diffusion v1.5 LDM with U-Net $\epsilon_\theta(\mathbf{z}_t, t, c)$ operating on latents $\mathbf{z}_t$ obtained from the VAE encoder $\mathcal{E}$. Text condition $c$ is provided by CLIP.

**Detection Adapter.** Multi-scale intermediate features $\{F_l\}$ are extracted from selected U-Net decoder blocks at a fixed denoising timestep $t^\star$. A small FPN-style adapter $g_\phi$ projects them to detection features:
$$
\hat{F} = g_\phi(\{F_l(\mathbf{z}_{t^\star}, t^\star, c)\})
$$
A standard detection head (e.g., Faster R-CNN style) then predicts boxes and classes from $\hat{F}$. Only $g_\phi$ and the head are trained; the LDM weights are frozen.

**Training the adapter.** Real annotated images $\mathbf{x}$ are encoded to $\mathbf{z}_0=\mathcal{E}(\mathbf{x})$, partially noised to $\mathbf{z}_{t^\star}$, and the detection loss
$$
\mathcal{L}_{\text{DA}} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{cls}} + \mathcal{L}_{\text{reg}}
$$
is back-propagated only through $g_\phi$ and the detection head.

**Data engine pipeline.** Given a class vocabulary, prompts are sampled (templated and LLM-paraphrased). Stable Diffusion generates images; the trained DA produces pseudo boxes on the same latent features used to synthesize the image, ensuring perfect feature/image alignment and avoiding the noise of post-hoc detector annotation. Optional confidence and CLIP-similarity filters discard low-quality samples.

**COCO-DE / VOC-DE.** Datasets sized to several times the original training sets, with class-balanced prompt sampling to mitigate long-tail effects.

## Results

- **COCO**: pretraining/augmenting with COCO-DE yields consistent +1.5 to +3.1 AP improvements across Faster R-CNN, RetinaNet, FCOS, DINO and YOLOX detectors; gains are larger in low-data regimes.
- **VOC**: VOC-DE adds +2 to +5 mAP, especially under label-efficient (10–25%) settings.
- **Cross-domain**: training on DE-generated data transfers competitively to unseen target domains.
- **Ablations**: choice of timestep $t^\star$ in the mid range works best; multi-scale adapter clearly beats single-scale; CLIP filtering provides modest extra gains.

## Limitations

- Class distribution in generated data depends on prompt sampling; rare/compositional layouts remain hard.
- Detection quality is bounded by Stable Diffusion's image realism and known failure modes (counting, small objects).
- Adapter is trained on real boxes, so the engine inherits the biases of the seed annotated set.
- Single-stage generation cannot enforce strict scene layouts the way ControlNet-based pipelines can.
